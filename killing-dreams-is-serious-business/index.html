I like the sound of typing. I like the way the keys make their clicks and then pause. I try to stay aware of little things how they relate and connect. I love the connectedness of things and how disparate seemingly inconsequential variables can have huge effects with subtle variances. I find this fascinating. I think about exponential ideas and I try to turn it on itself and get smaller and smaller and smaller.
 
There was this video that was going around about the sizes of different celestial objects and it shows Sol then Andromeda and on and on to VV CELPHEI and on… and I LOVED how small it made me feel. I loved how massive and huge these things were and just how little we are. I sometimes imagine zooming out from where I am sitting and seeing the street and the city and the world then space and then nothing…. A lot of people are terrified of the great vast nothingness… but I find some kind of solace or comfort there. I find some peace in that thereis some vast empty - that such emptiness must exist in contrast to our existence. I know about agoraphobia and I’m definitely not that - I’m definitely more claustraphobic… but I just love the idea of being truly insignificant. When Yuki was dying… I thought of the suns hurdling through space at breakneck speeds with planets chasing - scraping their orbits in graviatational violence. I thought about the massive majesty of everything - and how everything was moving in concert and all the atoms and all the solar systems spining spinning spinning - and I thought about how insignificant she was in the grand scheme of things… but how - at that moment - she was literally everything to me and that I’d stop every fucking atom - every twitching nuclei - if I could - just to have another peaceful moment with her.

The second loss is hard.. because it’s when we re-remember our memories and start tainting them with greif. We lose the purity of the before memories by injected our new understanding into them with our supposed “new found” “understanding”. The second loss is sometimes qualified as the sounds and the smells and all of those things, but really - it’s how we recolor our memories to suit our new futures. It is the hardening that can happen if we define things in a self destructive way. Grief is dangerous. Grief is not your friend. It is not something to rise out of. It is an energy and it consumes.
 
Some will say is love with nowhere to go - - - - and I used to believe that and maybe I still do… but there is vengence in grief. There is rage. The big feelings that seek to hold the whole court accountable - wobbly table legs and all.
 
I don’t know what i’m in these dark places tonight. I don’t like to tarry here - as the air is thicker and everything is slower… but grief… if you are in grief… is dangerous. it is pervasive and seeks out small places and crawls with you into your darkness. It scuttles and creeps… grief is not the burden or the cost or any accounting…. it just is - and it is random and makes no sense. I barely grieved my father - who I miss every day and think of with smiles and joys - but other griefs are aggressive and can steal and take and wretch…

Understanding and labeling - dissecting the grief is painful - but important. Making choices of memory is important. Choosing which memories you will carry. Choosing the you in your mind you choose to be. To characterize yourself as you wish to be.
